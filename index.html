Youth Activism & Climate Justice

YouthRising

How young people are leading the fight for climate justice.

Climate change affects everyone, but not everyone is affected equally. Around the world, young people are organizing, striking, educating, suing, and pressuring leaders to act.

A guided story in six parts
01 / The Problem

Climate change is not equal.

Climate justice means putting fairness, human rights, and future generations at the center of climate decisions.

The people who contributed least to climate change are often harmed the most.

Source: UNDP, 2023

3.3–3.6billion

people live in contexts highly vulnerable to climate change.

Source: IPCC, 2022

Climate risks are highest where poverty, weak infrastructure, and inequality overlap.

Source: IPCC, 2022

02 / Unequal Impact

Who pays the price?

Climate change does not hit every community the same way.

03 / The Solution

Climate justice means solving the crisis fairly.

Real solutions must work at individual, community, and government levels.

Individual

  • Build climate literacy.
  • Share verified climate information.
  • Attend school board or city council meetings.
  • Join a youth climate organization.
  • Teach peers or younger students.
  • Help translate climate resources.

Sources: UNESCO; Climate Cardinals; Earth Guardians

Community

  • Start a school climate club.
  • Run a school energy or waste audit.
  • Plant trees and create shade.
  • Support cooling centers and disaster preparedness.
  • Advocate for public transportation and walkability.
  • Make climate resources multilingual.

Sources: National Wildlife Federation; EPA; Climate Cardinals

Government

  • Transition to clean energy and green jobs.
  • Create youth seats on climate councils.
  • Require climate education in schools.
  • Fund green infrastructure and fair disaster relief.
  • Hold governments and polluters legally accountable.
  • Protect Indigenous land rights.

Sources: UNESCO; EPA; Our Children’s Trust; One Earth

04 / Pressure

How young people turn concern into change.

Youth activism works because it turns private fear into public pressure.

Protest

Youth strikes and marches make climate inaction visible.

Source: Fridays for Future

Education

Young people teach others about climate justice, climate science, and community action.

Source: UNESCO; Climate Cardinals

Legal Action

Youth lawsuits argue that governments must protect future generations.

Source: Our Children’s Trust

Language Access

Translation helps more communities understand climate change and take action.

Source: Climate Cardinals

Frontline Organizing

Youth from affected communities lead with lived experience, culture, and local knowledge.

Source: Pacific Climate Warriors; Earth Guardians

05 / People

Solutions do not happen by themselves.

Young people turn climate problems into pressure.

06 / Movements

From individual voices to global movements.

Youth activism grows when personal action becomes organized pressure.

Fridays for Future

  • Started from Greta Thunberg’s 2018 school strike.
  • Youth-led global climate strike movement.
  • Uses school strikes, global protest days, and digital campaigns.
  • Pushes leaders toward science-based climate action.

Sources: Fridays for Future, “Who We Are”; Fridays for Future, “Our Demands”

Sunrise Movement

  • U.S.-based youth climate movement.
  • Connects climate action with economic and racial justice.
  • Pushes for Green New Deal-scale policy.
  • Uses direct action, electoral organizing, and movement-building.

Sources: Sunrise Movement, “About”; Sunrise Movement, “Green New Deal”

Our Children’s Trust

A nonprofit law firm that helps young people use the courts to fight for a stable climate.

  • Filed Juliana v. United States with 21 youth plaintiffs.
  • Uses rights-based climate lawsuits.
  • Shows that climate justice can be fought in courtrooms, not just streets.
  • Helped inspire youth climate litigation around the world.

Sources: Our Children’s Trust, “Juliana v. U.S. — Ten Years of Impact”; Our Children’s Trust, “Juliana v. United States”

Pacific Climate Warriors

A youth-led Pacific Islander network defending island homelands from rising seas and fossil fuel expansion.

  • Uses the message: “We are not drowning. We are fighting.”
  • Organizes across Pacific Island nations and diaspora communities.
  • Uses storytelling, culture, song, dance, and direct action.
  • Reframes Pacific Islanders as leaders, not helpless victims.

Source: 350 Pacific, “Pacific Climate Warriors”

About

About This Project

This project explores how young people are leading the climate justice movement. Instead of treating climate change only as an environmental issue, it shows how climate change connects to inequality, human rights, race, income, geography, Indigenous rights, and future generations. Through youth activists and movements, the project shows how young people use protest, policy, education, digital activism, lawsuits, and storytelling to demand change.
